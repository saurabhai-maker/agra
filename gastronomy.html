A 400-Year-Old Royal Sweet

How The Taj Mahal Gave Birth to Agra Petha

According to culinary legend, Emperor Shah Jahan tasked his royal master chef (Mir Bakawal) with inventing an instant energy-giving, pure vegetarian delicacy for the 20,000 artisans laboring under the scorching sun to build the Taj Mahal.

Made from white ash gourd (winter melon) boiled in pure sugar syrup and flavored with royal saffron, kewra water, and cardamom, Petha became Agra’s signature gift to the world. Today, legendary shops like Panchi Petha offer over 50 gourmet varieties including Kesar Petha, Angoori Petha, and Paan Petha.

A Day of Eating in Agra

The Royal 24-Hour Food Trail

Follow our food critic-approved timeline to taste the best street breakfasts, afternoon biryanis, and evening chaat strolls.

🌅 08:00 AM • MORNING RITUAL

Deviram Sweets

Head to Pratap Pura for freshly fried Bedhai (urad dal puffed puris) paired with spicy potato sabzi and hot golden spiral jalebis soaked in sugar syrup.

☀️ 01:30 PM • ROYAL LUNCH

Pinch of Spice or Peshawri

Dine on Fatehabad Road for fragrant Awadhi dum biryani, melt-in-the-mouth galouti kebabs, and rich mutton korma with buttery roomali roti.

🌆 06:30 PM • EVENING STROLL

Sadar Bazaar Chaat Gali

Walk the vibrant food alley in Sadar Bazaar for crunchy aloo tikki, dahi gujiya, tangy golgappe with spiced cumin water, and creamy kulhad lassi.

🌙 09:30 PM • SWEET FINALE

Panchi Petha Emporium

Finish with tasting boxes of Angoori (grape-shaped) petha soaked in saffron syrup, chocolate-coated petha, and take home crunchy Dalmoth lentil snacks.
